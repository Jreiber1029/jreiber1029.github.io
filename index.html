KOMA
Local AI Toolbox

Project documentation on GitHub ↗

What started as a solution to a practical problem became something much more. KOMA began as a vision-language model fine-tuned on construction data, housed in a toolbox alongside an air quality monitor. The aim was to explore whether continuous safety monitoring could run on a portable device with limited computing power.

Developing the prototype taught me about the constraints of building AI systems and the importance of open-source software and openly available models, including Qwen 3.5 VLM 2B. It also raised a challenge: a construction-specific safety system is difficult to demonstrate meaningfully in an exhibition, while access to construction sites for testing is limited.

I began using the toolbox itself as a storytelling medium. KOMA now explores a speculative future where access to open AI models is restricted to select people and uses. Its fictional history draws parallels with the concentration of ownership and erosion of privacy on today’s internet, asking what is at stake when people lose the ability to run and adapt AI for themselves.

The system brings together a custom YOLO26n model, a fine-tuned vision-language model, speech recognition and a custom text-to-speech voice. Its extensive retrieval-augmented knowledge base (RAG) draws on local health and safety legislation and guidance, alongside the history and characters of the speculative world, so KOMA can respond in context.

At the exhibition, visitors can scan one of two ID cards to start a conversation with KOMA in English or Chinese. In Safety Manager mode, they can show KOMA a scene and ask about what it sees. Its fine-tuned vision-language model draws on the RAG’s local health and safety legislation and guidance to offer context-aware safety advice.

Work in progress — nearing completion.

PPES
Personal Protection
and Exposure System

A wearable prototype designed to give workers greater awareness of their environment. PPES combines air quality sensing with camera-based PPE classification on an Arduino UNO Q.

When readings cross a threshold, distinct audible alerts and an LED matrix signal the detected hazard. The system checks whether protective equipment is being worn and records sensor readings, exposure events and PPE status for review through a local dashboard.

Aerobic Bioreactor
Simulator

An exploration of decomposition through a constructed environment. The aerobic bioreactor simulator brings organic matter, sensors and exposed electronics together in a glass enclosure, with a display showing temperature and humidity.

The project is accompanied by Manifesto for Responsible Digestion, an owner's manual for the simulator.

Audio DEV C3
TinyML Audio +
Air Quality Recorder

A portable recorder for building TinyML training datasets from workplace sounds and air quality. Built around an ESP32-C3, it pairs microphone recordings with measurements from a SEN66 environmental sensor.

Physical buttons let the user label activities such as drilling, sanding and grinding, then record audio alone or alongside environmental readings. The device works offline, saving timestamped WAV audio and Edge Impulse-compatible CSV data to a microSD card for training models to recognise hazardous workplace conditions.
